HOA and city garage sale rules for agents running a neighborhood sale

Before the first flyer goes out, make sure the sale is allowed and you know the rules on permits, hours, and signs. Here is what to check and who to ask.

See the kit Get the free guide

By Clark Richardson. Last updated November 10, 2026.

This page is general information, not legal advice. Rules are different in every HOA and every city, and they change. Always read your HOA's governing documents and check your own city or county rules before you announce a sale.

The short answer: there are usually two sets of rules to check. The HOA's governing documents and board decide whether a community sale can happen in the neighborhood and how. The city or county decides whether permits are needed, how often a home can hold a sale, what hours are allowed, and where signs can go. As the agent organizing it, you also need to follow your brokerage's advertising rules and the consent rules for any follow up.

What to check with the HOA

An HOA's rules usually live in its governing documents, such as the declaration or CC and Rs, the bylaws, and any rules adopted by the board. Ask the board or the management company for the current versions, and ask them directly:

  • Is a community garage sale allowed, and does the board need to vote on it?
  • Are there rules on sale hours, parking, or using common areas?
  • Where can signs go inside the community, and for how long?
  • Can the HOA share the sale in its newsletter, email list, or resident portal?
  • Is the board comfortable with an agent organizing it, and with a sign up form that asks residents if you may contact them afterward?

Get the board's approval in writing, even if it is a short email. If the HOA says no, or the neighborhood has no HOA, you can still organize a sale among willing neighbors, as long as you follow the city's rules.

What to check with the city or county

Look on your city's website or call its planning, code enforcement, or permits office. The questions that matter most:

  • Does each home need a garage sale permit, and is there a fee?
  • Is there a separate option for a community or neighborhood sale?
  • How many sales can one address hold per year, and how many days can a sale last?
  • What hours are allowed?
  • Where can signs go, how big can they be, and when do they have to come down?
  • Are there rules about selling food or certain items?

If your area is unincorporated, the county sets these rules instead of a city.

A real example: San Antonio, Texas

To show how detailed city rules can be, here is what the City of San Antonio lists on its garage sale page, checked November 10, 2026. A permit is required for garage, yard, and estate sales within city limits, with a $20 fee. Each residence can hold four sales per calendar year, one per quarter. Sales run between 9 a.m. and 6 p.m. A community garage sale for an apartment complex or HOA group must be held in a common area. Three signs are allowed, none larger than 6 square feet, and signs cannot go on utility poles, street signs, or other objects in the city right of way. Signs can go up two days before the sale and must come down when the permit expires.

Other cities have no permit at all, and some have different limits. That is exactly why you check your own city instead of assuming.

Sign and flyer basics

  • Never put flyers inside mailboxes. The U.S. Postal Service says mailboxes are only for postage paid mail, and unstamped flyers in them are against federal law. Leave flyers at the front door instead.
  • Keep signs off utility poles and traffic signs unless your city clearly allows it.
  • Put your name and phone number somewhere on your materials so neighbors know who is organizing.
  • Take every sign down when the sale ends. It goes a long way toward keeping the board's trust.

Rules that apply because you are an agent

  • Brokerage advertising rules. Ask your broker how branded flyers and signs should look and whether they need approval. States have their own rules for how licensees advertise.
  • Consent for follow up. Add a clear checkbox to the sign up form asking if you may contact people after the sale, and respect anyone who says no or later opts out.
  • Do Not Call. The National Association of REALTORS notes that agents generally may not cold call a number on the National Do Not Call Registry to solicit a listing. Businesses can register to access the registry through the FTC. When in doubt, use email with consent, or ask your broker about your office's policy.

Questions we hear

Is a permit needed for a community garage sale?

It depends on your city or county. Some require a permit for every home, some have a community sale option, and some require nothing. Check your city's website or call its permits office.

Can an agent organize a sale without an HOA?

Yes. Without an HOA, you organize willing neighbors and follow the city's rules on permits, hours, and signs.

Who is responsible for the permit?

That depends on the city. In some places, only the owner or resident of each property can get the permit. Ask your city who should apply.
